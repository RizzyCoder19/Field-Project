## 1. FACTUAL CONCLUSIONS (EMPIRICAL FINDINGS)

Based on the forensic audit and statistical analysis of 539 baseline outbreak records (2022–2025; 21,955 reported cases; 266 reported deaths) and 45 out-of-sample comparison records (2026 W01–W32) from official NCDC/IDSP surveillance archives:

1. **Observed Seasonal Concentration in Vector-Borne Disease Reports:**
   - **Dengue** demonstrates a pronounced seasonal concentration in reported events during the second half of the calendar year. The June–October window encompasses 82.8% of all reported baseline outbreak events, with peak notification activity recurring between August and October ($S_m = 235.3$ in October).
   - **Malaria** exhibits a tightly constrained early-monsoon seasonal concentration. The contiguous May–July window accounts for 51.4% of all reported baseline outbreaks ($S_m = 257.1$ in June).

2. **Reproducible Enteric Elevation with Outlier Vulnerability:**
   - **Acute Diarrheal Disease (ADD)** displays the highest inter-annual rank concordance among all studied diseases (Kendall's $W = 0.530$, $p = 0.016$, statistically significant under FDR control), with regular event elevations during the peak monsoon months of June through August (43.1% of events).
   - However, reported case volumes for enteric diseases are heavily distorted by isolated high-case contamination events (e.g., a single 1,000-case outbreak in Nanded district).

3. **Outlier-Driven Point-Source Dynamics:**
   - **Food Poisoning** outbreak events do not follow continuous meteorological cycles. Instead, they exhibit bimodal peaks in late winter (January–February, 26.1%) and late spring (April–May, 29.0%).
   - Case distributions are dominated by rare high-case outbreak events; three records in Kolhapur (651 cases), Parbhani (629 cases), and Solapur (335 cases) accounted for 1,615 cases in February alone, representing an extreme outlier / case-volume effect.

4. **Extreme Spatial Focalization:**
   - Statewide Malaria surveillance patterns are driven predominantly by two eastern Vidarbha districts—Gadchiroli (38.6%) and Chandrapur (22.9%)—which account for 61.4% of all reported baseline outbreaks in Maharashtra.
   - Chikungunya exhibits urban reporting concentration in Pune district (25.6% of events), whereas Dengue and ADD display broad reporting across more than 30 districts.

5. **Surveillance Data Asymmetry & Event-Case Disconnect:**
   - Outbreak event frequency and reported patient volume measure fundamentally different public health dimensions. Event counts reflect surveillance detection sensitivity across administrative units, whereas case volumes reflect cluster severity. Conflating the two produces misleading epidemiological conclusions.

## 2. EVIDENCE-GROUNDED PUBLIC HEALTH RECOMMENDATIONS

> [!NOTE]
> **Surveillance Logistics Scope:** The following recommendations are strictly operational and administrative public health measures. They do not constitute clinical treatment guidelines, pharmacological directives, or individual medical advice.

### Recommendation 1: Operational Pre-Positioning Windows
Public health authorities in Maharashtra should transition from reactive post-outbreak response to calendar-informed proactive logistics:
- **Malaria (April–May Pre-Positioning):** Rapid diagnostic test (RDT) kits and vector control resources should be fully positioned across primary health centres in Gadchiroli and Chandrapur districts by **April 30**, preceding the recurrent May–July outbreak surge.
- **Dengue (July–August Mobilization):** Municipal vector-control squads, source-reduction campaigns, and hospital fever bed capacities across urban and semi-urban districts (Pune, Thane, Kolhapur, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) should be activated in **early August**, preceding the primary September–October peak notification window.
- **Acute Diarrheal Disease (May Water Audits):** District water sanitation engineering teams should conduct pipeline pressure checks, chlorine tablet distribution, and well disinfection ahead of the **June–August** monsoon runoff period.

### Recommendation 2: Dual-Track Surge Protocols (Events vs. High-Volume Outliers)
Surveillance monitoring systems should implement distinct operational tracks:
- **Community Outbreak Response:** Scaled for frequent, moderate-sized clusters (10–50 cases), common in Dengue and localized ADD outbreaks.
- **High-Case Incident Protocol:** Dedicated rapid-containment workflows for rare, high-volume institutional clusters (>300 cases), such as institutional water supply contamination or large point-source food poisoning incidents.

### Recommendation 3: Targeted Spatial Surveillance Focus
State epidemiological resources should reflect empirical spatial burden:
- Intensify dedicated vector surveillance in forested eastern Vidarbha rather than uniform statewide allocation for Malaria.
- Address reporting asymmetries in Chikungunya by standardizing laboratory confirmatory notification protocols across non-metro district hospitals to resolve current surveillance sparsity.

## 3. EXPLICIT METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS

To maintain complete academic defensibility during viva examination, the following limitations must be explicitly acknowledged:

1. **Surveillance Threshold Bias:** IDSP data capture investigated outbreak events that meet official notification thresholds. They do NOT reflect endemic primary-care patient visits, outpatient department (OPD) attendance, or asymptomatic community transmission.
2. **Denominators & True Incidence:** The dataset does not contain population at risk or total health-seeking denominator data. Consequently, calculating true incidence rates, prevalence rates, or relative risk per 100,000 population is methodologically invalid.
3. **Government Archive Gaps:** In the 2023 calendar year, 49 weeks were published. Weeks 15, 51, and 52 were unpublished / unavailable in the public NCDC archive (Week 50 is present and published). No synthetic zeros were inserted into the dataset.
4. **Dominant-Year Surges:** Specific diseases are heavily weighted by single years of intense surveillance activity (e.g., Dengue in 2023 accounts for 51.0% of baseline records; ADD in 2024 accounts for 46.4%). Multi-year averages must be interpreted with this weighting in mind.
5. **Absence of Direct Meteorological Co-Modeling:** Weather variables (rainfall depth, relative humidity, ambient temperature) were not linked to individual outbreak records. Therefore, climatic causation cannot be asserted; seasonality is demonstrated strictly as calendar-month empirical concentration in reported events.
6. **2026 Truncation:** 2026 surveillance records represent a 32-week partial year and cannot be used to evaluate annual totals or forecast unobserved late-year (Weeks 33–52) outbreak behavior.
