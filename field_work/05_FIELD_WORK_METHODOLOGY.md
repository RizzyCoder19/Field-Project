## 1. FIELD-WORK TITLE
*Community and Public-Health Perspectives on Seasonal Disease Occurrence, Reporting, and Municipal Response in Maharashtra: A Contextual Qualitative Framework Supporting Secondary Epidemiological Surveillance Analysis.*

---

## 2. BACKGROUND & RATIONALE
Government surveillance systems, specifically the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP) under the National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), provide apex-level epidemiological data by aggregating weekly outbreak notices across administrative districts. However, secondary quantitative datasets inherently lack micro-level operational context:
- They record outbreak clusters that cross formal reporting thresholds, but do not capture community-level health-seeking behaviors that precede formal notifications.
- They identify temporal peaks (such as the June–October concentration in Dengue or the February case spikes in Food Poisoning), but cannot directly measure local sanitation friction, public awareness, or municipal response dynamics.
- They document "Suspected" versus "Confirmed" diagnostic labels, but do not reveal the frontline laboratory bottlenecks experienced by rural Primary Health Centres (PHCs) or urban municipal dispensaries.

Integrating a rigorous qualitative field methodology provides the necessary empirical grounding to interpret statistical findings without overstepping analytical boundaries.

---

## 3. RESEARCH PURPOSE
The primary purpose of this field-work component is to collect primary contextual and qualitative evidence regarding:
1. Community perceptions of seasonal disease timing and environmental triggers.
2. The operational flow of disease notifications from households and private clinics to public surveillance machinery.
3. The perceived efficacy and timeliness of municipal public health interventions.
4. The practical feasibility of public-facing seasonal risk alerts.

This study does **not** attempt to calculate population disease prevalence or statistically validate state-level epidemiological rates; it functions strictly as **qualitative contextual evidence**.

---

## 4. CORE RESEARCH QUESTIONS
The field inquiry is structured around four primary qualitative research questions:
- **RQ-FW-1:** *How do community residents, pharmacists, and local healthcare workers perceive the timing and onset of major seasonal illnesses (Dengue, Malaria, ADD, Food Poisoning, Chikungunya) compared to the calendar patterns observed in state surveillance data?*
- **RQ-FW-2:** *What environmental, infrastructural, and behavioral factors are locally identified as primary drivers of seasonal outbreak vulnerability?*
- **RQ-FW-3:** *What pathways, informal channels, and systemic bottlenecks govern the notification of localized disease clusters to municipal and IDSP health authorities?*
- **RQ-FW-4:** *How do frontline actors perceive the responsiveness of municipal preventive measures (e.g., fogging, larvicide, water quality monitoring), and how could data-driven early warning systems aid local preparedness?*

---

## 5. SPECIFIC FIELD OBJECTIVES
1. Administer structured questionnaires to residents and frontline workers across diverse settlement typologies in Maharashtra.
2. Conduct semi-structured key-informant interviews with healthcare professionals, chemists, community health workers (ASHA/ANM), and municipal personnel.
3. Perform systematic environmental audits of drainage, domestic water storage, and sanitation conditions using standardized observation rubrics.
4. Document the presence and quality of public-health Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials.
5. Create an auditable, transparent primary evidence register linking field notes, audio recordings (where consented), and non-sensitive contextual photographs.

---

## 6. TARGET PARTICIPANT CATEGORIES
To ensure multi-perspective triangulated qualitative evidence, participants are categorized into seven distinct stakeholder groups:
1. **General Community Residents:** Household decision-makers, long-term residents (>3 years in locality), evaluating perceived illness timing and domestic water practices.
2. **Community Health Frontline Workers (ASHA / ANM / Anganwadi):** Frontline monitors tracking fever registers, distributed ORS/chlorine tablets, and community notification.
3. **Primary Care Physicians (MBBS / BAMS / BHMS):** Neighborhood General Practitioners (GPs) who act as the first point of clinical contact for acute febrile and diarrheal episodes.
4. **Retail Pharmacists / Chemists:** High-frequency observers of OTC antipyretic, anti-diarrheal, and antibiotic sales shifts.
5. **Diagnostic Pathologists & Lab Technicians:** Frontline reporters of platelet drops, NS1 antigen positivity, and Widal/malaria slide tests.
6. **Institutional Administrators & Teachers:** Observers of absenteeism clusters, school mid-day meal operations, and hostel sanitation.
7. **Municipal & Public Health Officials:** PHC Medical Officers, Sanitary Inspectors, and Ward Vector Control Officers.

---

## 7. SAMPLING APPROACH & SITE SELECTION
- **Sampling Strategy:** Purposive and convenience sampling designed to capture socioeconomic and infrastructural diversity rather than demographic statistical representation.
- **Geographic Typologies:**
  - *High-density urban informal settlements / chawls* (elevated vector and water-supply vulnerability).
  - *Planned urban residential and commercial zones* (moderate drainage, private-clinic dominance).
  - *Peri-urban construction corridors* (transient populations, artificial water pooling).
  - *Rural gram panchayat communities* (groundwater dependence, agricultural fringe).
- **Target Sample Size Framework:**
  - Full Questionnaires: Target 30–50 completed forms.
  - Short/Rapid Questionnaires: Target 50–100 completed forms.
  - Key Informant In-Depth Interviews: Target 8–15 recorded/transcribed interviews.
  - Environmental Site Observations: Target 10–15 distinct location audit sheets.

---

## 8. QUESTIONNAIRE METHODOLOGY
- **Instruments:** Two standardized instruments are deployed:
  - `01_FIELD_WORK_QUESTIONNAIRE_FULL.md` (50 items): In-depth instrument for detailed interviews and key informants.
  - `02_FIELD_WORK_QUESTIONNAIRE_SHORT.md` (20 items): Rapid 5–8 minute instrument for high-volume community intercept surveys.
- **Administration Protocol:** Administered face-to-face by the student investigator in English, Marathi, or Hindi based on respondent fluency. Neutral, non-leading wording is strictly maintained.

---

## 9. SEMI-STRUCTURED INTERVIEW METHODOLOGY
- **Protocol:** Governed by `03_FIELD_INTERVIEW_GUIDE.md`.
- **Technique:** Open-ended conversational interviewing guided by 12 core thematic questions, supplemented by dynamic probing based on professional expertise.
- **Recording:** Digital audio recording is conducted solely upon explicit, signed/recorded participant consent. If audio is declined, rapid handwritten shorthand notes are taken and expanded immediately post-interview.

---

## 10. SYSTEMATIC OBSERVATION METHODOLOGY
- **Protocol:** Governed by `04_FIELD_OBSERVATION_CHECKLIST.md`.
- **Procedure:** Investigator conducts walking transects (100–200 meter radius) across selected observation nodes, recording drainage flow, mosquito breeding receptacles, drinking water pipe integrity, and municipal IEC signage.
- **Photographic Policy:** Contextual photographs are strictly restricted to public physical infrastructure, drainage, and informational posters. No identifiable patient faces or private residential interiors are captured without formal release.

---

## 11. INFORMED CONSENT & PRIVACY SAFEGUARDS
- **Voluntary Autonomy:** All participants are informed that participation is entirely voluntary and that declining will have no adverse consequences.
- **Anonymity by Design:** No personally identifiable information (Aadhaar numbers, full home addresses, phone numbers, exact clinical records) is collected or stored in project databases.
- **Participant Coding:** Every participant is assigned an alphanumeric code (e.g., `FW-RES-012`, `INT-DOC-003`). The master linking key is kept in an encrypted, isolated directory.

---

## 12. DOCUMENTATION & CHAIN-OF-CUSTODY PROCEDURE
Every collected field asset is indexed in `07_FIELD_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.csv` with a unique `evidence_id`, timestamp, GPS/locality tag, evidence classification (`PRIMARY` vs. `REFERENCE`), and verification status. Physical paper forms are cataloged in an indexed physical binder; digital notes, audio files, and photos are backed up to a secure local directory.

---

## 13. DATA ORGANIZATION & DIGITIZATION
Quantitative and categorical responses from field questionnaires are keyed directly into the standardized tabular schema `06_FIELD_RESPONSE_TEMPLATE.csv`. Data entry incorporates automated integrity checks (range validation on age, categorical dropdown constraint matching).

---

## 14. QUALITATIVE DATA ANALYSIS APPROACH
Qualitative field data is analyzed using **Thematic Analysis** following Braun and Clarke's framework:
1. *Familiarization:* Transcribing audio and repeatedly reviewing field observation logs.
2. *Initial Coding:* Tagging segments of text with descriptive codes (e.g., `[DRAIN_STAGNATION]`, `[PRIVATE_CLINIC_LEAKAGE]`, `[REACTIVE_FOGGING]`).
3. *Thematic Synthesis:* Grouping codes into the 7 analytical themes defined in `09_FIELD_FINDINGS_FRAMEWORK.md`.
4. *Triangulation:* Comparing community narratives against environmental audit scores and official NCDC surveillance profiles.

---

## 15. RELATIONSHIP TO SECONDARY NCDC/IDSP ANALYSIS
The methodological boundary between quantitative and qualitative components is absolute:

| Dimension | Primary Quantitative Surveillance (Phase 1–5E) | Primary Field / Qualitative Context (Field Work) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data Source** | Official NCDC / IDSP Weekly Outbreak Reports | Primary questionnaires, interviews, field audits |
| **Coverage** | State of Maharashtra (36 districts, 2022–2026) | Selected micro-localities and purposive informant cohort |
| **Nature of Data** | Hard epidemiological counts (records, cases, deaths) | Perceptions, operational friction, environmental context |
| **Analytical Role** | Measures temporal, spatial, and statistical patterns | Explains real-world mechanisms, reporting context, and public-health realities |
| **Inference Rule** | Drives all empirical statistical findings | Contextualizes findings; does **not** prove or modify them |

---

## 16. METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS
1. **Non-Probabilistic Sampling:** Convenience sampling restricts generalizability; findings reflect qualitative patterns in visited localities, not statistical state-wide averages.
2. **Recall Bias:** Community memories of illness timing are subject to cognitive heuristics, often conflating recent severe events with historical multi-year trends.
3. **Surveillance Disconnect:** Private medical practitioners may not be fully informed about district IDSP notification pathways, reflecting operational opacity rather than system failure.
4. **Cross-Sectional Temporality:** Field visits represent single seasonal snapshots, limiting direct observational tracking across full annual cycles.

---

## 17. ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS & COMPLIANCE PLEDGE
This research adheres to the ethical standards for academic undergraduate research established by the University of Mumbai:
- Zero fabrication: No simulated interviews, synthetic survey entries, or false field visits are permitted.
- Beneficence and non-maleficence: The study poses zero physical, legal, or psychological risk to participants.
- Neutrality: The investigator remains an objective observer and does not offer medical diagnoses or endorse private commercial health products.
