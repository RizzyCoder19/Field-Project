## 3. CORE INTERVIEW MODULE (12 QUESTIONS WITH ADAPTABLE PROBES)

### Module 1: Seasonal Chronology & Local Disease Onset
1. **Core Question 1:** *In your professional or daily experience in this area, how does the burden of acute illnesses shift across the year? In which specific weeks or months do you start seeing the first signs of an impending surge?*
   - *Probe for Clinicians/Pharmacists:* What is the lead indicator—is it an increase in sales of paracetamol, or sudden requests for platelet counts?
   - *Probe for ASHA/Community:* Does illness spike immediately when the first showers fall, or is there a 2- to 4-week delay while puddles stagnate?

2. **Core Question 2:** *The state surveillance data indicates that Dengue outbreaks cluster heavily between June and October, whereas Malaria appears somewhat earlier (May–July). Does this match what you see in this locality? How do the two illnesses behave differently on the ground?*
   - *Probe:* Do patients present with severe joint pains and prolonged recovery (suggesting Chikungunya) alongside Dengue?

3. **Core Question 3:** *Regarding gastrointestinal infections (vomiting and diarrhea), when do they become most critical here? Is it a summer water-shortage phenomenon, or an early-monsoon pipeline-contamination issue?*
   - *Probe:* How do sudden water contamination events manifest in community clusters versus scattered individual cases?

### Module 2: Outbreak Detection, Thresholds & Reporting Pathways
4. **Core Question 4:** *What triggers the recognition of an "outbreak" in your day-to-day work? How many cases in a single building, street, or school cause you to recognize that a clustered outbreak is underway?*
   - *Probe for Clinicians:* When you treat 5 patients with high fever from the same chawl or apartment, what is your next course of action?
   - *Probe for Institutional Staff:* At what point does school attendance alert you to a localized food- or water-borne issue?

5. **Core Question 5:** *How does information about a local disease cluster travel from the patient’s home to the government surveillance machinery (IDSP / Municipal Health Department)?*
   - *Probe for ASHA / ANM:* Walk me through your reporting routine. Do you report daily or weekly? Do you use physical registers, WhatsApp groups, or the IHIP (Integrated Health Information Platform) portal?
   - *Probe for Private Doctors:* Do private clinics actively report cases to the municipal ward medical officer, or does reporting happen mainly through diagnostic laboratories?

6. **Core Question 6:** *In government reports, many records are classified as "Suspected" rather than laboratory-confirmed. From your perspective, what are the primary challenges in securing laboratory confirmation during a rapid outbreak?*
   - *Probe:* Availability of rapid NS1 / IgM kits, cost barriers in private labs, or delays in transporting blood samples to district testing facilities?

### Module 3: Food Poisoning & Point-Source Events
7. **Core Question 7:** *Surveillance data indicates that Food Poisoning outbreaks often feature very high case numbers in single events (sometimes hundreds of cases at once). In this region, what kinds of events or conditions typically lead to such large cluster poisonings?*
   - *Probe:* Is it mass catering at social functions, institutional hostels, mid-day meals, or dairy/sweetmeat spoilage during extreme summer heat?
   - *Probe:* Why do food poisoning cases show sharp, isolated spikes rather than smooth seasonal trends?

### Module 4: Environmental Risk Factors & Preventive Interventions
8. **Core Question 8:** *What are the most persistent environmental breeding grounds or sanitary vulnerabilities in this ward/village?*
   - *Probe:* Stagnant open drains (nullahs), construction sites, decorative fountains, indoor water storage barrels (due to intermittent water supply), or uncovered overhead tanks?

9. **Core Question 9:** *When and how do municipal or gram panchayat authorities intervene? How would you characterize their response—is it proactive preventive intervention, or reactive containment after cases have already multiplied?*
   - *Probe:* How effective is chemical fogging versus indoor anti-larval abatement? Does the public cooperate when health workers inspect domestic water containers?

### Module 5: Public Communication, Data Use & Technology
10. **Core Question 10:** *How aware are ordinary residents about the real-time disease risks in their specific neighborhood? Where do people get their health information?*
    - *Probe:* Do public health posters and municipal advisories actually influence household behavior, or do people rely mostly on social media forwards and word-of-mouth?

11. **Core Question 11:** *If the public health department were to publish a weekly "Seasonal Disease Forecast" (similar to a weather report, warning that Dengue risk will spike in Ward X in 3 weeks), how would that impact your work or community preparedness?*
    - *Probe for Doctors/Pharmacies:* Would you pre-order testing kits and IV fluids in advance?
    - *Probe for Community:* Would residents proactively inspect water drums, or would it induce unnecessary alarm?

### Module 6: Systemic Bottlenecks & Closing Reflection
12. **Core Question 12:** *From your vantage point, what is the single biggest bottleneck in the current disease reporting and response pipeline?*
    - *Probe:* Lack of personnel, delayed lab reporting, fragmented private-sector data, or insufficient inter-departmental coordination between water supply and health wings?
