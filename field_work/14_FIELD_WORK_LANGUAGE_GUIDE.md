## 1. THE THREE STRICT EVIDENCE CATEGORIES
To maintain absolute scientific defensibility during project reviews and viva examinations, the candidate must categorize all statements regarding field work into one of three distinct lexical categories. **Under no circumstances may these categories be blurred or conflated.**

```
========================================================================================
CATEGORY A: PRIMARY EVIDENCE ACTUALLY COLLECTED
- Used ONLY when empirical field evidence is completed, documented, and registered.
- Grounded strictly in physical field notes, signed forms, and verified audio/photos.
========================================================================================
CATEGORY B: REFERENCE FIELD EVIDENCE
- Used when citing published government manuals, municipal reports, or academic studies.
- Grounded in secondary public-health literature and operational guidelines.
========================================================================================
CATEGORY C: PLANNED FIELD WORK / METHODOLOGICAL DESIGN
- Used when describing instruments, proposed sample frames, or scheduled visits.
- Grounded in research design, intended fieldwork, and pre-deployment protocols.
========================================================================================
```

## 2. STANDARDIZED PHRASEBOOK BY EVIDENCE CATEGORY

### Category A: Primary Evidence Actually Collected (Past/Empirical Tense)
*Use these phrases ONLY after field questionnaires, interviews, or observations have occurred and are logged in `07_FIELD_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.csv`:*

- *"During the field interaction in Ward X, the respondent stated that..."*
- *"Direct physical observation of the stormwater drainage canal revealed..."*
- *"In our semi-structured interview with a local community health worker, it was noted that..."*
- *"Field transect audits conducted across three residential blocks documented..."*
- *"Responses collected via the rapid field questionnaire indicate that 65% of surveyed residents..."*
- *"Frontline chemists in the visited market cluster reported an increase in OTC antipyretic sales beginning in..."*
- *"Qualitative thematic analysis of informant transcripts identified reporting friction as..."*

### Category B: Reference Field Evidence (Attributed Analytical Tense)
*Use these phrases when discussing external government manuals, municipal SOPs, or peer-reviewed qualitative papers:*

- *"Published field investigations by the National Institute of Epidemiology indicate that..."*
- *"According to the IDSP District Surveillance Officer Operational Manual, an outbreak is defined as..."*
- *"Municipal health bulletins published by the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai document that..."*
- *"Reference operational guidelines from the NVBDCP emphasize that pre-monsoon source reduction should occur in..."*
- *"Peer-reviewed public health literature from Maharashtra suggests that private clinic reporting leakage occurs because..."*
- *"Standard operating procedures from FSSAI indicate that point-source food poisoning investigations require..."*

### Category C: Planned Work / Methodological Design (Future/Hypothetical Tense)
*Use these phrases currently while field work is designed, pre-registered, and awaiting physical deployment:*

- *"A 50-item master field questionnaire has been developed to investigate..."*
- *"The qualitative research design incorporates purposive sampling across four settlement typologies..."*
- *"Semi-structured key-informant interviews are planned to explore frontline notification bottlenecks..."*
- *"Standardized observational rubrics have been established to audit environmental vector breeding conditions..."*
- *"If deployed across selected urban wards, this instrument is designed to capture..."*
- *"The proposed fieldwork aims to complement the secondary NCDC quantitative dataset by providing..."*
- *"Field deployment protocols have been standardized under strict institutional ethical guidelines..."*

## 3. STRICTLY FORBIDDEN PHRASES & HOW TO REWRITE THEM

The following table lists dangerous or academically dishonest phrases, explains why they are prohibited, and provides the defensible replacement:

| Prohibited Phrase (DO NOT USE) | Why It Is Unacceptable | Defensible Academic Alternative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"We visited hospitals across Maharashtra and proved that Dengue peaks in July."* | Fabricates unconducted hospital visits and confuses qualitative interviews with statistical proof. | *"Our secondary analysis of NCDC surveillance data shows Dengue peaks between June and October, and our planned field interviews are designed to observe how local clinics experience this timing."* |
| *"Our survey proved the government under-reports cases by 80%."* | Commits mathematical overclaiming; convenience surveys cannot calculate true epidemiological multipliers. | *"Qualitative interviews with local clinicians highlight significant reporting friction, suggesting that formal IDSP surveillance captures severe outbreak clusters rather than mild community cases."* |
| *"Every person we interviewed said the municipality does nothing."* | Over-generalized anecdotal hyperbole; lacks professional academic tone. | *"Field interviews explore community perceptions of municipal responsiveness, distinguishing between proactive pre-monsoon interventions and reactive containment."* |
| *"We collected data from all 36 districts in Maharashtra."* | Mathematically impossible for a student field component; conflates secondary NCDC dataset with primary fieldwork. | *"The secondary NCDC surveillance dataset covers all 36 districts of Maharashtra (2022–2026), whereas the primary field work focuses on selected micro-localities for contextual qualitative grounding."* |
| *"Field work confirmed that rainfall caused the disease outbreak."* | Asserts direct biological/climatic causation without meteorological co-modeling or lab confirmation. | *"Field observations documented extensive water accumulation in blocked drains, providing visible physical context for the seasonal concentration observed in vector-borne disease reports."* |

## 4. VIVA DEFENSE SCRIPTS FOR THE CANDIDATE

### Scenario 1: The Examiner asks, *"Did you actually go to the field, or is this just an internet project?"*
> **Candidate Answer (Clear, Honest, Confident):**  
> *"Sir/Madam, this project is built on an end-to-end research methodology. The primary quantitative analysis is based on 809 apex government outbreak records from the National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC/IDSP) from 2022 to 2026, which we extracted and rigorously audited.*  
>  
> *To fulfill the university's Field Project vision, we designed a complete qualitative field-work framework—including a 50-item master questionnaire, a rapid 20-item survey, and environmental observation rubrics. At this stage, the field instruments and ethical protocols are fully standardized and ready for deployment. We are completely transparent that our current empirical results come from the secondary government surveillance dataset, while the field framework provides the operational bridge to ground those findings in community reality."*

### Scenario 2: The Examiner asks, *"Why did you not combine your survey responses with the NCDC dataset?"*
> **Candidate Answer (Demonstrating Data Science Maturity):**  
> *"That was a deliberate methodological decision to avoid an ecological fallacy. The NCDC dataset represents complete administrative surveillance of official outbreaks across 36 districts, whereas our field survey uses purposive, non-probabilistic qualitative sampling.*  
>  
> *Statistically pooling them would violate sampling theory because the units of analysis and denominators are entirely different. Instead, we use field work as qualitative contextual evidence to explain the human, clinical, and administrative processes that generate the numbers in the government spreadsheets."*
